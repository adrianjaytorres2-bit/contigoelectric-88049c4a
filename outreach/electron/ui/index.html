Guide
What everything means, in plain English.


      

        
⭐ Quality score — the one to understand

        
Every audited site gets a quality score from 0 to 100. It measures how badly the website needs work — not how good it is.

        

          

          

            0
Polished site
Poor prospect
            50
Some issues
            100
Broken / outdated
Great prospect
          

        

        
High score = good for you. A high score means the site has lots of real problems (no mobile support, broken layout, outdated design), which means more to fix and an easier sale. A low score means the site is already clean — nothing to pitch, so it's a weak lead.

        
The threshold (Settings → “Min. quality score to email”): the app only emails leads scoring at or above your threshold. Anyone below it is marked skipped — “too good to bother.” Default is 40. Lower it to email more leads; raise it to only chase the worst sites.

        
💡 The email was already written before the skip decision, so overriding a skipped lead costs nothing extra — you're just choosing to send an email that's already done.

      


      

        
The 6-step pipeline

        
    
          
  1. Load leads — import a CSV (name, email, company, website). Duplicates are removed automatically.
    2. 
          
  2. Audit — opens each website invisibly, screenshots it, and measures real flaws (mobile, speed, SEO, outdated content). Uses no AI credits — it's free.
    3. 
          
  3. Generate drafts — Claude reads the screenshot + flaws and writes a personal email per lead. This is the only step that costs money (see below).
    4. 
          
  4. Send — goes out from your own mailbox, spaced apart, capped per day. Dry run shows the email without sending.
    5. 
          
  5. Follow-ups & replies — nudges people who didn't answer; “Check inbox” pulls replies and sorts them by intent.
    6. 
          
  6. Report — a full HTML overview of everyone in the pipeline.

💵 What it costs

        
Only the Generate drafts step uses paid AI — auditing, sending, follow-ups, and reply-sorting are effectively free (sorting a reply is a fraction of a cent).

        
Writing one personalized email (reading the screenshot + flaws, then drafting) runs roughly 1.5–3¢ per lead on Sonnet 5, or about 4–5¢ on Opus. So a batch of 100 leads costs a couple of dollars. That's very cheap for researched, personalized outreach — a person doing the same by hand takes 5–10 minutes each.

        
💡 Skipping low-value sites (the quality threshold) and using the cheaper model are your two biggest cost levers.

      


      

        
Overriding skipped leads

        
On the Dashboard, if any leads were skipped for scoring below your threshold, a “Send to skipped anyway” button appears under step 4. It re-queues those already-written emails so the next Send includes them. Leads skipped because their site was broken/parked have no email to send, so they stay skipped.

      


      

        
Email voice, length & editing

        
Settings → AI (email writing) has 7 voice presets (Natural, Casual, Professional, Direct, Story-Driven, Witty, Consultative) and 3 length options. Every voice already avoids em dashes, AI clichés ("I hope this finds you well," "unlock," "seamless"), and the "not just X, it's Y" pattern that makes outreach read as machine-written — that's built in regardless of which style you pick.

        
Want full control over one specific email? Go to the Emails page — every drafted subject and body is editable right there. Change anything, hit Save changes, and it sends exactly as you wrote it. Already-sent emails are locked (nothing to edit, they're gone).

        
💡 HTML formatting is optional and off by default. Plain text usually reads as more human and can deliver better for cold outreach — worth testing both.

      


      

        
When the AI picks the wrong issue

        
Sometimes the AI fixates on one weak or irrelevant flaw instead of the real problem with the site. On the Emails page, each drafted email has a "Tell the AI what to actually focus on" box underneath it — type the real issue(s) you noticed yourself, hit 🔄 Redraft with these notes, and it rewrites the email prioritizing exactly what you said over its own judgment. Your notes are saved per lead, so they're remembered if you redraft again later.

        
💡 This is different from directly editing the email text — redrafting still goes through the AI (so it stays in your chosen voice/length/style), it's just now aimed at the right target.

      


      

        
Adding something you found after drafting

        
Found something extra after the email was already written — a bad review, a news mention, anything relevant? Below the redraft box on the Emails page is a second one: "Found something else? Paste it and add it to this email." Paste in whatever you found, hit ✨ Summarize & add to draft, and the AI turns it into one natural sentence and weaves it into the existing email — everything else stays as it was, it's a small targeted edit, not a full rewrite.

      


      

        
Follow-ups & bounce protection

        
Before every single follow-up, the address is re-verified. The original email may have gone out weeks ago, and domains expire and mailboxes get shut off in the meantime — sending a nudge to an address the app can already tell is dead is a second avoidable bounce against a sender reputation you're still building. Anything that fails is dropped from the sequence automatically instead of being retried.

        
If a send actually bounces, that lead's follow-ups stop immediately — on the first send too, not just on follow-ups. When the server explicitly says the mailbox doesn't exist, the address is also permanently suppressed. When it's a vaguer rejection (spam filter block, mailbox full), follow-ups still stop but the address is kept, since those can happen to perfectly real prospects and silently burning one would be worse.

        
To remove a lead by hand, open its 🔍 Details on the Leads page and hit ⏸️ Stop follow-ups. The lead stays in your list with its full history — it just stops getting nudges. The same button turns into ▶️ Resume follow-ups to undo it, and paused leads show a ⏸️ no follow-ups badge in the table.

        
💡 This is different from 🚫 Unsubscribe, which blocks the address from all email permanently and can't be re-found by Find Leads. Stopping follow-ups is narrower and fully reversible.

      


      

        
Deleting a lead you don't want

        
Reading a draft and decided the lead isn't worth pursuing? Each email card has a 🗑️ Not worth it — delete lead button at the bottom, in its own row so it can't be mis-clicked alongside the save buttons. It removes that one lead and its draft after a confirmation. Works for already-sent emails too, if you just want to clear them out.

        
💡 Deleting does not stop the lead reappearing in a future Find Leads run for the same area. If you never want to see it again, use 🚫 Unsubscribe this lead from the lead's Details panel on the Leads page instead — that keeps it permanently blocked.

      


      

        
Viewing a site & searching drafts

        
Every drafted email on the Emails page has a 🌐 View site button next to its status badge — opens the actual website in your regular browser (not inside the app) so you can look something up while reading the draft. A search bar above the list filters by name, company, email, subject, or body, so you can jump straight to one lead in a long list.

      


      

        
Leads with no website

        
Check "Only businesses with no website" on the Find Leads page to hunt specifically for businesses that have no site at all. This is an exclusive search — anything with a website is thrown out, so you get a completely different list from a normal search rather than a longer one. Having zero web presence is often a stronger pitch than a bad website, so this can be the highest-value list you can build.

        
A match with an email gets a dedicated "you don't have a website" pitch instead of the usual audit-based one (there's nothing to audit, and nothing gets invented). A match with only a phone number is still added to your Leads list but can't be auto-emailed — it's flagged so you know to call it yourself.

        
⚠ On free OpenStreetMap data a missing website is often just an unmapped field rather than a business that genuinely has none, so expect false positives. A Google Places API key gives far more reliable results here. Also expect mostly phone-only leads — businesses without a website usually don't list an email either.

      


      

        
Adding leads manually & one-off emails

        
On the Leads page: ➕ Add lead adds a single person by hand (no CSV needed) — they come in as “new” and go through the normal Audit → Generate drafts → Send pipeline like anyone else.

        
✉️ Quick send skips the pipeline entirely — write a subject and body yourself and it sends immediately from your configured mailbox. No audit, no AI, no queue. Use it for a one-off note to someone you already know, or to follow up outside the automated flow. It still records in your Leads list (marked “sent”) so your history stays complete.

      


      

        
Unsubscribe handling

        
Every email sent by this app — drafts, follow-ups, and Quick Sends — automatically gets a footer: "Reply 'unsubscribe' and you'll be removed immediately." If your Check inbox step sees a reply asking to be removed, that address is permanently suppressed — it will never be emailed again by this tool, even if it's re-imported, re-found by Find Leads, or added manually later.

        
You can also suppress or unsuppress anyone by hand from a lead's ✏️ Notes/tags panel on the Leads page.

        
💡 Add your business's physical mailing address in Settings — commercial email law (like CAN-SPAM) expects one in the footer.

      


      

        
Notes, tags & bulk actions

        
Click ✏️ Notes/tags on any lead to jot context ("called, left voicemail") and add tags (comma-separated) for your own organization — search on the Leads page matches tags too.

        
Select multiple leads with the checkboxes to bulk-export them to CSV or bulk-delete them. Export all leads to CSV at the bottom of the page dumps your whole list (name, email, company, status, score, notes, tags, reply intent) for backup or use elsewhere.

        
Importing, Find Leads, and manual add all warn when a company name looks like a near-duplicate of an existing lead (e.g. "Bob's Plumbing LLC" vs "Bobs Plumbing Inc"), so you don't accidentally email the same business twice under different contacts.

      


      

        
Warmup mode

        
Turn this on in Settings when you're on a new domain or mailbox. Instead of using your full daily send cap from day one, it starts low (e.g. 5/day) and steps up automatically every few days until it reaches your target cap. The Dashboard and Analytics always show today's actual cap, which is enforced automatically by Send and Follow-ups — you don't have to remember to raise it yourself.

      


      

        
A/B testing

        
Turn this on in Settings and pick two voice styles (Variant A / Variant B). When you generate drafts, each lead is stably assigned one variant — the split is roughly 50/50, and re-drafting the same lead keeps its variant so results stay comparable. Check Analytics for the reply-rate comparison once you've sent a reasonable batch (a handful of sends won't tell you much — look for dozens per variant before trusting the numbers).

      


      

        
Scheduling a send

        
The 🕒 Schedule button (Dashboard, step 4) queues your current drafted batch to send automatically at a chosen time, with three reasoned presets — or pick a custom time. The app has to stay open for the schedule to fire; it's not a background service. If you close the app before the scheduled time, the send is still queued and will run (or catch up) the next time you open the app.

      


      

        
Email verification (reducing bounces)

        
Every email is checked automatically right before drafting (free — syntax, whether the domain even has a mail server, and known throwaway/disposable domains). Addresses that fail are marked skipped with the reason and never get a draft written, so no AI cost is wasted on an address that was always going to bounce. Role addresses like info@/contact@/sales@ are not filtered — those are normal, actively-read small-business inboxes, exactly what Find Leads is supposed to find.

        
Add a deep-verification API key in Settings → Email verification to also check whether the actual mailbox exists — catches the case free checks can't (a real domain with a dead inbox). On the Leads page, ✅ Check emails for bounce risk re-checks every address in your list at once, useful before a big send or for leads imported before this feature existed. A ✓ verified / ⚠ risky / ⚠ bounce risk badge shows next to each lead's status.

      


      

        
Preferring small/independent businesses

        
The Find Leads page has a "Prefer small/independent businesses" checkbox that filters out chains and franchises using three signals: OpenStreetMap's franchise/brand tags, businesses appearing at more than one location in the same search (a strong chain signal even without any tags), and an AI check of each remaining business name for recognizable national/regional chains. Nothing is hardcoded — the AI check works for any business type or brand, the same way business-type search already generalizes. Add a max review count (Google Places searches only) to also skip anything with a big review count, another common chain signal.

      


      

        
Themes

        
Settings → Appearance has 17 color themes — from the classic 🌙 Midnight Amber to 🔵 Steel Blue (matches the AT DEV GROUP logo), plus fun ones like 🖥️ Matrix, 🌆 Cyber Neon, 📼 Vaporwave, 🧛 Dracula, and two light modes (🌅 Daybreak, 📜 Parchment). The whole app recolors live as you browse the dropdown; your pick is saved when you hit Save settings and sticks between launches.

      


      

        
Checking for updates

        
The 🔄 Check for updates button at the bottom of the sidebar checks for a newer version on demand — nothing runs in the background. It shows live status right there: checking, downloading with a percentage, "you're on the latest version," or an error if it can't reach the update feed. When a new version finishes downloading, an ⬇️ Update ready — restart button appears; click it to install and relaunch. Updating only works in the installed app (the portable .exe you re-download by hand).

      


      

        
Facebook DMs

        
The Facebook DMs page is a second, separate outreach channel from email — for businesses whose website links to a Facebook Page (found automatically during Find Leads). Click Generate DM drafts to write a short, casual Messenger-style message per eligible lead, referencing the same real flaw found in their audit.

        
This never sends anything for you. Facebook has no legitimate API for messaging a business you have no relationship with, and automating your own account to send DMs would violate their Terms of Service and risk it getting banned. Instead: click Open Facebook Page to pull up their Messenger, Copy message to grab the draft, paste it in yourself, and hit send. Then mark it Sent (or Skip) to move to the next one. 10–15 real messages a day, sent by you, is completely normal Messenger use.

      


      

        
🔒 Security

        
Your Anthropic API key, SMTP/IMAP passwords, and Google Places key are encrypted at rest using your operating system's secure storage (Windows Credential Manager, macOS Keychain, or Linux libsecret) — not saved as plain text. This ties them to this specific computer and user account, which is expected: they won't transfer if you copy your settings file to another machine.
